Synth engines T1 page of a synth sound

Shift + T1 picks the engine. Shift + turn is a fine adjustment on every engine. The T2 page is the same ADSR on all of them: blue attack, green decay, white sustain, orange release; with shift held it shows the play mode (poly / mono / legato / unison), portamento, bend range and volume.

Engine Technique Blue Green White Orange
clusterCls.cppstock Multi-layered oscillator cluster. Up to six oscillators stacked on one note, spread apart in pitch, each with its own level envelope. "Unitor" pulls the layers together into one unison voice. Additive/unison synthesis rather than subtractive: there is no filter. wave number: how many oscillators sound (0 to 6) wave envelope: the envelope shape applied per layer spread: pitch spacing of the layers unitor: unison amount
digitalT10.cppstock "True digital synthesis". A digital oscillator run through a wave shaper, with a second oscillator that can be detuned against it or ring-modulated with it. "Digitalness" degrades the signal towards lo-fi (bit and sample-rate reduction) by the sound of it. Inferred from the labels and the result; the source names only say T10. wave shaper: the shaping curve on the oscillator octave: the second oscillator's octave detune, and ring modulation on/off at the end of the range digitalness: lo-fi degradation
dnaId.cppstock Wavetable oscillator with a modifier, noise and a filter. A numbered wave, bent by a "modifier" (a wave-shaping or phase operation on the table), mixed with noise and sent through a filter. Inferred from the labels; the twin-helix screen just draws the two parts. wave number: which wave wave modifier: bends the wave noise: noise mixed in filter
dr waveDrWave.cppstock "Frequency domain synthesis", described by TE as a raw 8-bit style engine. A short single-cycle wave table whose type and length are chosen together, so short tables give the coarse, aliased 8-bit character. A filter, a phase offset, and a chorus on top. wave type and length filter phase chorus
dsynthdsynth.cppstock Dual-oscillator synth with cross modulation, the drum engine dbox's architecture for the keyboard. Two oscillators with selectable waveforms and their own envelopes; oscillator 2 modulates oscillator 1 (cross modulation) and the pair goes through a filter. Shift: blue frequency of oscillator 2, green its waveform, white its envelope, orange filter cutoff. env crossfader: blends between the two envelopes waveform of oscillator 1 envelope of oscillator 1 cross modulation amount
fmFm.cppstock Four-operator FM synthesis. Four sine operators in a selectable algorithm ("topology"); frequency sets the operator ratios, FM amount the modulation index, detune spreads the operators. topology: the operator algorithm frequency: operator ratio fm amount: modulation depth detune
phasePd.cppstock Phase distortion, the Casio CZ method. Confirmed in the code: an 8192-entry wave table read through a phase accumulator whose phase is bent (shifted, tilted) before the lookup, so timbre comes from distorting the phase rather than filtering. A simple filter after it. In this firmware the phase slot also hosts Rings (the MULTI engine below): an orange click on the engine page swaps the two. phase shift distortion amount phase filter phase tilt
pulsePls.cppstock Dual pulse-train oscillator. Two pulse waves; the second's level and the pulse-width modulation are the colour controls, with a filter in front. filter amplitude second pulse: level of the second train modulation: pulse-width modulation
stringSt.cppstock Waveguide string model (Karplus-Strong). Confirmed in the code: at note-on the string's delay line is filled with white noise at full level (an LCG random generator), then it rings down through the loop. Tension sets the loop's filtering (stiffness/brightness), decay the loop gain, impulse the excitation shape. tension decay detune impulse: the pluck's character
voltageSlump.cppstock Analogue-style oscillator with voltage "slump". The labels describe a VCO emulation: a modulation source, a "ground noise" floor mixed in, a phase filter and detune. The internal name Slump suggests the sagging, imperfect voltages the engine imitates. Inferred. modulation ground noise phase filter detune
samplerSynthSampler.cppstock Chromatic sample player with a loop region. Plays from start, loops the in/out region while the key is held, plays through to the end on release. Tap an encoder to zoom around its point. Shift: blue direction, green fine tune, white loop fade, orange gain. start loop in loop out end
iterIter.cpphidden A complete engine (type 14) that the stock menus never list; op1repacker's "iter" mod adds the menu row. Not enabled in this firmware. Its knob functions aren't documented anywhere we trust. ????
MULTI: Ringsports/rings (phase slot, orange click)added Mutable Instruments Rings: physical-modelling resonator, six models. 1 modal: a bank of band-pass resonators (up to 60 modes across the voices) struck by the key, structure spacing them inharmonically. 2 sympathetic strings: Karplus-Strong strings, two per voice, resonating with each other. 3 inharmonic string: one string with dispersion and modulation. 4 FM voice: two-operator FM with feedback. 5 quantized sympathetic strings: the chord table (Bryan Noll's). 6 string + reverb: strings into a shared reverb (polyphony 4). Polyphony "all" is six voices; 1 / 2 / 4 trade voices for detail (more modes or strings per voice).

Drum modules drum mode, T1

Shift + 1-8 loads a kit. A drum kit is the engine (T1), the drum envelope (T2), an effect (T3) and an LFO (T4). The drum envelope is not an ADSR but a transient shaper: blue attack, green gain, white release, orange timing (the original calls it smooth). Every key has its own sound.

ModuleTechniqueBlueGreenWhiteOrange
drum sampler"drum"stock Per-key sample slicer. One sample per kit; each key plays a region of it with its own pitch, play mode (once / loop / hold) and direction. Tap white or orange to zoom on the in/out point. Shift: blue direction, green fine tune, white loop fade, orange gain. tuning / pitch of the key in point out point play mode: once, loop, hold
dbox"dbox"stock Dual-oscillator drum synth. Two oscillators per key with selectable waveforms and pitch envelopes; oscillator 2 cross-modulates oscillator 1; a filter on the output. The same architecture as the dsynth engine. Shift: blue pitch of oscillator 2, green its waveform, white its envelope, orange filter cutoff. pitch of oscillator 1 waveform of oscillator 1 envelope of oscillator 1 cross modulation amount
Gridsports/grids (pattern sequencer, drums)added Not a sound module but the drum sequencer this firmware adds: see Sequencers. It plays the kit loaded here through the normal drum keys (BD key 0, SD key 2, HH key 8 by default; reassignable). ----

Effects T3 page of a sound; mixer T3 on the master

One effect per sound (and one on the master mix). Shift + T3 picks it, T3 toggles it. A switched-off effect costs nothing: the firmware doesn't call it. Effects run at 44.1 kHz on 64-sample blocks. Shift + turn is a fine adjustment.

EffectTechniqueBlueGreenWhiteOrange
cwoBodeAudioEngine.cppstock Frequency-shifting delay. The source file is named after Harald Bode, inventor of the frequency shifter, and TE described it as a frequency shifting delay made with Magnus Lidström (Sonic Charge). A frequency shifter (not a pitch shifter: every partial moves by the same number of hertz, so harmonics go inharmonic) inside a feedback delay. "Sideband" picks the upper or lower sideband mix of the shifter. The cow on screen eats the sound through four stomachs. frequency: the shift in hertz delay time feedback sideband: upper / lower sideband balance
delayDssd.cppstock "Solid state delay". A plain feedback delay with a one-second buffer. Size and speed set the time in two stages (a coarse range and a rate within it, which is why the time can be swept like a tape delay); feedback and mix as usual. size: coarse delay range speed: delay rate within the range feedback mix
gridGrid.cppstock "Three dimensional feedback plate". A lattice of short delays fed back into each other, a plate-reverb-like network whose three sizes set the delay lengths along the x, y and z axes. Harsh at high feedback; the usual advice is to keep the mix low. x size y size z feedback mix
nitroFtwo.cppstock "Dual resonant turbo filter". Two resonant filters (the source name says "filter two"). Blue sets the cutoff, green makes it follow the input level (an envelope follower, the "turbo"), white adds resonance / feedback and orange sets the second filter's frequency. frequency filter follow: envelope-follower amount resonance (feedback) frequency of the second filter
phonePtch.cppstock "Hacked telephone system": a pitch shifter dressed as a phone line. The source file is called Ptch, pitch. Tone is a band-pass colouring (the phone line), phonic is the pitch shift, baud degrades the signal like a low bit-rate codec (sample-rate / bit reduction) and telematic adds the line's artefacts. This is the frequency movement you hear; it is pitch shifting (all partials scale), not the frequency shifting of cwo. In this firmware the phone slot also hosts Clouds: an orange click on the FX page swaps the two. tone phonic: pitch shift baud: codec-style degradation telematic: line artefacts
punchMllp.cppstock "Hard hitting low pass filter". A resonant low-pass filter with an envelope-driven sweep: punch sets how hard the cutoff kicks on each hit, rounds how many times it bounces, power the drive into it. A filter that behaves like a compressor-sweep combination; TE's own tutorial uses it to add punch to drums. frequency: cutoff punch: envelope kick rounds: repeats of the kick power: drive
springRymd.cppstock "Mathematic reverb": a modelled spring reverb. Rymd is Swedish for space. A spring-line reverb model: tone colours the input, turns is the length of the spring (decay), damping the high-frequency loss, send the amount into it. It has audible self-noise, which goes to tape with it. tone turns: decay length damping send (mix)
filterMmmf.cpphidden A multi-mode filter effect present in the firmware (type 5) with its own screen, but with no row in the factory effect list, so the menu never offers it. op1repacker's "filter" mod adds the row. Not enabled in this firmware. ????
Cloudsports/clouds (phone slot, orange click)added Mutable Instruments Clouds: granular texture synthesizer, as a synth, drum or master effect. The input is written into a buffer; in granular mode many short grains are read out of it at a chosen position, size, pitch and density, overlapping into a texture; in looping delay mode the buffer is replayed as a delay with a pitch shifter. Both modes feed a diffuser, feedback and a reverb. Freeze stops recording so the texture holds the last moment. The core runs at 22.05 kHz between half-band filters to fit the CPU.

Page 2: blue texture (grain window / filter colour), green dry/wet, white feedback, orange reverb. Stereo spread is fixed at 50%. A CPU governor trims the grain count when the processor is short; with Rings at polyphony "all" it is, so keep Rings at 4 voices next to Clouds.		 position: where in the buffer grains are read size: grain length (looping delay: loop length) pitch: -24 to +24 semitones, dead zone at 0 density: grain rate; left of centre sparse and random, right of centre dense and regular

LFOs T4 page of a sound

One LFO per sound, modulating a knob of the engine, the envelope or the effect (or pitch and volume for some). Shift + T4 picks it, T4 toggles it. The "destination" knob picks the module and "parameter" the knob within it.

LFOTechniqueBlueGreenWhiteOrange
tremoloLfoDual.cppstock Dual LFO on pitch and volume. One oscillator with two depths, pitch and volume, either of which can go negative to invert the shape. It rewrites the voice's per-sample pitch increments and gain directly. Shift: blue speed fine, orange LFO shape (sine, saw, exp, square, blip). speed pitch amount volume level pitch envelope
valueLfoSingle.cppstock Single-destination LFO with four shapes. Square, ramp, saw or sine (tap blue to change), either retriggered by each note or free-running on the tempo, into one chosen knob. Shift: blue speed fine. speed amount destination: engine, envelope or effect (sync / free) parameter: which knob
randomLfoRnd.cppstock Stepped random (sample and hold) with smoothing. Draws a new random target at the speed and glides to it; the "envelope" shapes the glide. Applies to all four knobs of the destination at once (it keeps eight random targets and smoothed values internally). Stock random never reaches pitch; it modulates engine, envelope or effect knobs. speed amount destination envelope: smoothing of the steps
elementLfoReRoute.cppstock External-source modulation ("re-route"). Not an oscillator: the modulation is the g-force sensor, the envelope, the sum of what reaches the output, or an audio input (mic, line, radio, USB), scaled and sent to a knob. source: g-force, envelope, sum, input amount destination parameter
midiLfoMidi.cppstock MIDI CC to knob. Four incoming control changes (CC 1 to 4) each drive one internal knob. Shift: the four destinations. parameter 1 parameter 2 parameter 3 parameter 4
bendLfoBend.cppstock Pitch-bend as a modulator. Takes the bend input (the MIDI pitch wheel; it also writes the voice's pitch increments directly) and routes it to a knob or to pitch. Its orange knob accumulates rather than sets, which is why it behaves like a wheel. Original OP-1 only; the knob roles here are read from the firmware's ranges and are less certain than the rest. speed / range amount destination (engine, envelope, effect, pitch) bend: the accumulating bend value
crankLfoCrank.cppstock Hand-cranked LFO. The oscillator advances only when you turn the blue encoder (its value accumulates), so you play the modulation by hand. It can reach pitch or volume as well as the three modules. Original OP-1 only. crank: turn to advance amount destination parameter (pitch or volume when the destination is 3)

Sequencers shift + sequencer key

One sequencer per instrument (synth or drum), toggled with the sequencer key. All of them run from the keys: hold a key or chord to run, press shift while holding to latch (hold), the next key takes over and transposes, the last key up stops. The ones this firmware adds follow the same rule since seq14.

Marblesports/marbles (tombola slot, synth)added Mutable Instruments Marbles: random sequencer with memory. A random rhythm generator (T: three gates from a coin toss, a divider, drum patterns and other models, synced to the tempo) and random voltages (X: quantized to a scale) play notes: gate 1 with voltage 1, gate 2 with 2, gate 3 with 3, so they overlap into chords; a smooth voltage sets velocity. Deja vu loops the randomness: off is fresh each step, on repeats the loop with the amount of variation set by the knob, locked repeats it exactly. Pitch source: scale (the key you hold is the root, the voltages spread over two octaves) or keys (the held chord is the pool and X picks from it). Two pages, T and X.

Sources and confidence

Knob names are Teenage Engineering's (the stock sequencers' labels are listed in the guide's order, left to right): the OP-1 guide (teenage.engineering/guides/op-1, its PDF) and the OP-1 manual 1.2 reference tables (cluster, string, digital, pulse, fm, dr wave, phase, sampler, delay, nitro, grid, phone, punch, spring). The current guide is written for the OP-1 field; where the original's labels differ (delay's size/mix, drum envelope's smooth, phone's phonic/telematic) the original's are used, and the field-only modules (dimension, vocoder, mother, terminal, fazer, velocity LFO) are left out because OS 246 doesn't have them.

Techniques for the stock modules combine TE's one-line "type" descriptions, the firmware's own source-file names (recovered from its assertion strings: Fm, T10, DrWave, St, Cls, Pls, Pd, Id, dsynth, Slump, Iter; Dssd, Grid, Mllp, Ptch, Rymd, Mmmf, Ftwo, BodeAudioEngine; LfoDual, LfoSingle, LfoRnd, LfoReRoute, LfoMidi, LfoBend, LfoCrank) and what the disassembly confirms (phase's distorted-phase wavetable; string's noise-burst excitation; the master drive's parameter path). Rows that say "inferred" are informed reading of the labels, not verified code.

Added modules are described from their source in this repository (ports/rings, ports/clouds, ports/marbles, ports/grids) and the decisions in docs/.
