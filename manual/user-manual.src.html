OP-1 246 Beta Manual

A firmware for the original OP-1 that adds Mutable Instruments' Rings, Clouds, Marbles and Grids, nine extra effects, and the iter synth and filter effect. This manual covers everything this firmware adds. The rest of the OP-1 works as in Teenage Engineering's guide.

At a glance

  • multi in the synth list holds two engines: phase and Rings, a physical-modelling resonator with six models.
  • Four effect categories each hold several effects: reverb, dirt, modulation and delay. The orange click steps through the effects in a category.
  • Ten effects in those categories beyond the stock ones: plate, hall and Clouds (reverb); drive, tape, fold and crush (dirt); ensemble and shift (modulation); tape echo (delay).
  • Two sequencers: Marbles, a random sequencer with memory, for synths; Grids, a topographic drum sequencer, for drums.
  • The iter synth with 11 presets, and the filter effect.
  • Each effect remembers its settings while you switch between the effects of a category.
  • The tape screen draws the four tracks at the top of the display.

Install and recovery

The firmware installs through the OP-1's own update menu (te-boot). That menu starts before the firmware does, so you can always put the stock firmware back the same way. Installing this firmware leaves the update menu itself untouched.

  1. Back up anything you can't lose: in disk mode, copy your tape, sounds and albums to a computer.
  2. Switch the OP-1 off. Hold COM while switching it on. The te-boot menu appears.
  3. Choose 1. Upload firmware and connect USB. The OP-1 shows up as a disk.
  4. Copy op1_246_beta4.op1 onto it and eject. The OP-1 installs the file and restarts.
To go back to stock: first read Good to know, then the same steps with the stock op1_246.op1. Don't use te-boot's Factory reset or Format drive: they also erase the display's calibration files.

Basics

Everything is played with the four encoders. These modules also use clicks: press an encoder in.

  • Orange click steps to the next engine, effect or sequencer sharing that place in the list (phase → Rings, spring → plate → hall → clouds, and so on). Shift + orange click steps back.
  • The orange dots in the top-left corner of a screen show which one is playing. The first dot is the Teenage Engineering original.
  • The blue dots below them, on Rings, Clouds, Marbles and Grids, show the model or mode. The blue click changes it.
  • Other clicks are per module: white usually turns the page on two-page screens, and green freezes or holds where a module can. Each module's card lists them.
  • Shift + turn is a fine adjustment, as everywhere on the OP-1.
  • The screens follow the LFO: a knob the LFO moves, moves on screen too.
  • Settings memory. Every synth sound, drum kit and the master remembers the settings of every effect in a category while the OP-1 is on. Leave plate for hall and come back, and plate is as you left it. An effect you haven't used since switching on starts at its defaults. Only the effect showing is saved with the sound.

Synths

Shift + T1 picks the engine. Phase and Rings share the multi entry in the list; the orange click switches between them.

Rings

Mutable Instrumentsmulti: phase → rings

Mutable Instruments Rings, a physical-modelling resonator. Each key strikes a virtual resonator: a bank of tuned modes, a set of strings, or an FM voice. The sound rings out, darker or brighter, longer or shorter, depending on the knobs. Six models:

  • 1 modal: a bank of resonant modes, like a struck bar, bell or plate. Structure spaces them from harmonic to inharmonic.
  • 2 sympathetic strings: pairs of strings that resonate with each other.
  • 3 inharmonic string: one stiff string with dispersion.
  • 4 FM voice: two-operator FM with feedback.
  • 5 quantized sympathetic strings: the sympathetic strings tuned to chords.
  • 6 string + reverb: strings into a shared reverb.
  • structuremode spacing or string coupling, per model
  • brightnessexcitation brightness and how many modes sound
  • dampingdecay time
  • positionwhere the string or plate is struck
blue click: next model (shift: previous). The six blue dots show it.
green click: polyphony all (6 voices) / 1 / 2 / 4, shown by the green ticks. Fewer voices give each voice more detail.
orange click: phase ↔ Rings

iter

Teenage Engineering

A Teenage Engineering synth engine, with 11 community presets from op1.fun in its own preset folder: crystal pad, ebullition, feu doux, iter, korder, lotremkords, pulse bass, rezpiano, wonky bass, woodblock and wood bell. Its knobs have no official names; the screen shows what each one does as you turn it.

Effects

Shift + T3 picks the effect, and T3 switches it on and off. Each sound, each drum kit and the master (mixer T3) has one effect. The four categories each hold several effects; the orange click steps through them. A switched-off effect costs no CPU.

Reverb

spring, a modelled spring reverb: blue tone, green turns (decay), white damping, orange send.

plate

two pages

A classic plate reverb (the Dattorro design): a big steel sheet ringing. Wide, dense, bright and immediate, slightly metallic.

Page 1

  • dampbright to dark tail
  • pre-delay0–200 ms gap before the tail
  • mixdry to wet
Page 2
  • sizesmall plate to huge plate, in 8 steps
  • diffusionaudible early echoes to a smooth wash

green click: freeze. The tail holds forever and new sound stays out; the star turns green.

orange click: next reverb (shift: previous)

hall

two pages

A hall reverb: a space rather than an object. Smoother and softer than the plate, and its size goes from a small room to a huge hall.

Page 1
  • sizeroom to hall
  • decay0.3–20 s; the top of the knob is infinite (∞)
  • damphow much faster the highs die than the lows
  • mixdry to wet
Page 2
  • pre-delay0–200 ms
  • earlyearly reflections against the late tail
  • motiona moving, chorused tail
  • widthmono to wide (master only)
green click: freeze
white click: page 1 ↔ 2
orange click: next reverb (shift: previous)

At infinite decay with sound still coming in, the tail keeps building until it saturates. That's by design: freeze it, or lower the decay.

clouds

Mutable Instrumentstwo pages

Mutable Instruments Clouds, a granular texture processor. The sound is written into a buffer. In granular mode, many short grains are read out of it at the position, size, pitch and density you set, overlapping into a texture. In looping delay mode, the buffer replays as a pitch-shiftable delay. Both feed a diffuser, feedback and a reverb.

Page 1
  • positionwhere in the buffer the grains are read
  • sizegrain length (looping delay: loop length)
  • pitch−24 to +24 semitones, with a dead zone at 0
  • densityleft of centre: sparse and random; right: dense and regular
Page 2
  • texturegrain window and filter colour
  • dry/wetdry to wet
  • feedbackhow much of the output goes back in
  • reverbreverb amount
blue click: looping delay ↔ granular, shown by the first two blue dots. The other two dots are modes that aren't available yet.
green click: freeze: recording stops, so the texture holds the last moment
white click: page 1 ↔ 2
orange click: next reverb (back to spring; shift: previous)

Stereo spread is fixed at 50%. When the processor is short of time, Clouds plays fewer grains rather than crackling.

Dirt

punch, a hard-hitting low-pass filter: blue frequency, green punch, white rounds, orange power. The other four dirt screens share one layout: the input wave, the shaper, and what comes out.

drive

Overdrive and distortion, from gentle warmth to hard crunch. The level stays about the same as the drive goes up.

  • drive0 to +36 dB into the clipper
  • tonecentre is flat; left darker, right brighter
  • biassymmetric (buzzy, like a fuzz) to lopsided (warmer, like a valve). The valve on screen heats up as you turn it.
  • mixdry to wet
orange click: next dirt effect (shift: previous)

tape

A tape machine in a box: warm saturation like recording hot to tape, a low head bump, and the pitch wobble of an uneven transport. Run a synth or the whole mix through it, and it sounds like it came off a worn cassette.

  • saturateclean to compressed and warm
  • wobbleslow, deep wow at the bottom of the range; fast flutter at the top
  • agenew tape to worn tape: the highs roll off
  • mixdry to wet
orange click: next dirt effect (shift: previous)

fold

A wavefolder, the West Coast synth trick. Instead of clipping the peaks off, it folds them back, so a louder sound grows extra wiggles: bright, metallic and vocal. Best on a synth. The level is only roughly matched, so quiet input comes up and hot input goes down at high fold.

  • foldhow many folds
  • symmetrycentre folds both sides; off centre folds only the top or the bottom
  • tonedark to bright, tames the fizz
  • mixdry to wet
orange click: next dirt effect (shift: previous)

crush

A bit crusher, from subtle 12-bit grit to 2-bit destruction.

  • ratesample rate, 44.1 kHz down to 500 Hz: gritty, metallic aliasing
  • bits16 down to 2 bits, in steps: noisy and steppy
  • jitteran unsteady sample clock, like a dying sampler
  • mixdry to wet
orange click: next dirt effect (back to punch; shift: previous)
modulation · cwo → ensemble → shift

Modulation

cwo, a frequency-shifting delay: blue frequency, green delay time, white feedback, orange sideband.

ensemble

Stereo chorus and string ensemble. With one voice per side it's a classic chorus, wide and shimmering. With three voices per side it becomes the lush, swirling sound of 1970s string machines. Mix at 50% is the classic chorus; mix at 100% is pure vibrato.

  • rate0.05–8 Hz
  • depthhow far the voices drift
  • voiceschorus (one per side) to ensemble (three per side, a little quieter)
  • mixdry to wet
orange click: next modulation effect (shift: previous)

shift

two modes

Frequency shifter: moves every frequency up or down by the same number of hertz, so the harmonics stop lining up. Small shifts phase and shimmer; large ones turn clangorous and bell-like. With feedback the sound climbs or falls endlessly, like a barber pole. Ring modulator: multiplies the sound by a sine wave, for robotic, metallic voices. Ring mode is about 6 dB quieter.

  • shift−2 kHz to +2 kHz, fine near 0 (ring mode: the carrier, 0–2 kHz)
  • feedbackbarber-pole sweeps
  • spreadleft shifted up, right down (master only)
  • mixdry to wet
blue click: frequency shift ↔ ring mod (the arrow or the ring lights)
orange click: next modulation effect (back to cwo; shift: previous)
delay · delay → tape echo

Delay

delay, a clean feedback delay: blue size, green speed, white feedback, orange mix.

tape echo

A tape echo in the style of a Space Echo. Sound goes onto a tape loop and is played back by up to three heads, so the echoes repeat in a rhythm. Each repeat comes back a little darker, more saturated and more wobbly. Turn the time while it repeats, and the echoes bend in pitch like real tape. Turned up, the feedback self-oscillates into a dub howl.

  • time30 ms – 1.5 s, the spacing of the repeats (shift: fine)
  • feedbackhow many repeats; above about 0.9 of the knob it self-oscillates
  • agenew tape to worn: wow, darkening and drive per repeat
  • mixdry to wet
blue click: the head pattern: head 3, heads 2+3, 1+2+3, 1+3 (shift: back). The lit heads show it.
green click: hold. The input is cut and the tape loops what it holds; the loop turns green.
orange click: delay ↔ tape echo

Other effects

phone, grid and nitro are Teenage Engineering's originals, as described in their guide.

filter is a Teenage Engineering multi-mode filter with its own screen. Its knobs have no official names; the screen shows what each one does.

Sequencers

Shift + the sequencer key picks one. Marbles and Grids run from the keys like every OP-1 sequencer: hold a key or chord to run, press shift while holding to latch, the next key takes over, the last key up stops. The run starts on the key press, so it stays in time with the tape.

Marbles

Mutable Instrumentssynth · tombola → marbles

Mutable Instruments Marbles, a random sequencer with memory. A random rhythm generator (T) fires three gates, and random voltages (X), quantized to a scale, give each gate its note. Notes can overlap into chords. Deja vu loops the randomness: off is fresh every step, on repeats the loop with as much variation as the knob allows, locked repeats it exactly. The key you hold is the root.

Page T
  • rate1 bar to 1/32, with triplets
  • deja vuhow much the loop varies
  • jittertiming randomness
  • T biaswhich gates fire more often
Page X
  • spreadthe range of the notes
  • loop length1 to 16 steps
  • stepsall 12 notes, down to the root only
  • X biaslower or higher notes
blue click: the rhythm model (page T) or the scale (page X)
green click: the page's deja vu: off / on / locked
white click: page T ↔ X. Shift + white: the pitch source: scale (spread over two octaves from the root) or keys (the held chord is the pool of notes)
orange click: tombola ↔ Marbles

Grids

Mutable Instrumentsdrums · pattern → grids

Mutable Instruments Grids, a topographic drum sequencer. A map of 25 drum patterns (kick, snare and hat); moving around the map blends between them. Each part's fill sets how many of its hits play, and chaos perturbs the pattern. Accents come out louder, or on a key of their own. It plays the loaded drum kit: BD on key 0, SD on key 2 and HH on key 8 by default.

  • map Xacross the pattern map (shift: BD fill)
  • map Yup and down the map (shift: SD fill)
  • chaosrandom variation (shift: HH fill)
  • swing(shift: accent)

Euclidean mode: blue, green and white set the three parts' lengths (shift: their hits), orange the swing.

blue click: drums ↔ Euclidean (shift: 16ths ↔ 32nds)
green click: re-roll the chaos (shift: forget the accent keys)
white click: assign the next key you press to BD, SD, HH or their accents (shift: random voice on/off)
orange click: pattern ↔ Grids. Shift + orange: print the last bar into the pattern sequencer as steps you can edit

Tape

The tape screen draws the four tracks at the top of the display. Recording, playback and loops work as in Teenage Engineering's guide.

CPU

The OP-1 has one processor for everything. Most of these effects are light to medium. Clouds and Rings are the heavy ones, and together they're the combination to watch.

  • Rings next to Clouds: keep Rings at polyphony 4 or fewer. At "all" (six voices) with Clouds on the master, the OP-1 runs out of time and drops audio (pops, pumping, cut tails).
  • A switched-off effect costs nothing. The master effect is stereo and costs more than the same effect on a synth.
  • If you hear crackle, lower Rings' polyphony first, then move the heaviest effect off the master.

Good to know

  • Settings memory lasts until you switch off. A sound saves only the effect that's showing; after a restart, the others start at their defaults. Loading another preset into a sound doesn't clear what that sound remembers for its other effects.
  • Before going back to stock firmware, set every saved sound and drum kit to the first effect of its category (spring, punch, cwo or delay) and save it. Stock firmware checks the saved effect settings when it starts, and a sound saved on one of this firmware's effects might stop it from starting. If that happens, install this firmware again (COM at power-on), change those sounds, then install stock.
  • Factory presets play as Teenage Engineering made them.
  • Drive can sound slightly hollow at a half mix with low drive, because the driven signal arrives a fraction of a millisecond after the dry one.
