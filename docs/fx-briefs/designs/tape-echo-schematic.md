## Control map (changed)

| control | page 1 | page 2 (pressed) |
|---|---|---|
| blue | time (shift + blue: the tempo divisions, below) | input gain -6..+6 dB (word 5, shift for tenths) |
| green | feedback | hold (a latch: input off, feedback 1) |
| white | age | the head pattern (word 7, one click per pattern: 3; 2+3; 1+2+3; 1+3) |
| orange | mix | |

The head pattern was blue's page 2 in cs_tape_echo; blue's page 2 is the gain on every module now.
The heads are white for that reason.

**The tempo lock.** With shift held on the time page, each click of blue moves the time to the next
division of the project tempo: 1/16T, 1/16, 1/8T, 1/16., 1/8, 1/4T, 1/8., 1/4, 1/2T, 1/4., 1/2 (shortest
first; the first eight fit the 30 ms .. 1.5 s range from 40 to 300 BPM, the last three from 54, 60 and
80 BPM up, and the walk skips a division that doesn't fit). The first click goes to the nearest division beyond
where the time was; a turn without shift frees it. Each shifted click (a small change of the stored
time word, as the stock knob moves it under shift) is one step; the plugin then feeds the core the
division's time in place of the word (`TimeLock` in `tape_echo_knl.cc`) and never rewrites the word:
the stock knob keeps its own copy of a page-1 value and writes it back on the next click (Test 61
round 1, where only the divisions next to the knob's time could be reached). The lock is a performance
state, not saved with the sound. The tempo is the firmware's LFO tempo
increment (`knl::TempoIncrement`, `[0x1aadad4]` = bpm x 1662149.125, the stock 2.4.6 address; outside
20..400 BPM it's taken as 120 BPM). The heads sit at thirds of the time, so a 1/4 puts head 2 on a 1/4
triplet and head 1 on an 1/8 triplet; a dotted 1/8 puts them on an 1/8 and a 1/16.

## What it is

The Space Echo's signal path on the tape row (y 56), left to right: the input arrow, the record head,
the **tape** (a white wave, scaled by age about the row and breathing with the wow), the three **play
heads** with their taps down to a bus, the sum (a circle with a cross), the output arrow. The **return
path** runs under it (y 104) with green chevrons for the feedback. A blue **dot** travels the tape from
the record head to head 3, one trip per TIME (at most 8 a second); the heads sit 32 + 130 t px from
the record head (t = time 0..1), head 3 furthest, so a longer time spreads them. The blue
**input-gain gauge** at x 16 (rows 28..124, 0 dB at y 76), the orange **mix gauge** at x 304, the
**hold glyph** (a lemniscate) top right, the **readout** top left.

Static group `static`: the bus, the sum, the output, the tape's stub into the sum, the foot line.

## Gain in the plugin

The gain scales the decimated input before the core (saturating), so it is what reaches the record
head: more gain is more tape saturation and a louder echo, and the dry side of the mix is the input
as it came. The core is unchanged apart from two helpers for the time mapping
(`TapeEchoTimeSamplesQ4`, `TapeEchoTimeWord`, the time table and its inverse).
